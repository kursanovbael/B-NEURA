\*\*Primary Domain:\*\* Neurotechnology / Brain-Computer Interfaces / Virtual Reality / AI / Assistive Technology



\---



\# 1. PROJECT VISION



B-NEURA is a conceptual Neuro-VR system exploring how Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI), Artificial Intelligence (AI), Virtual Reality (VR), and sensory feedback could create new pathways between a person's intention and their experience.



The project focuses on people with severe paralysis who may retain cognitive intention despite having limited or no voluntary physical movement.



The central idea is:



> What if a person who cannot physically move could still control and experience a virtual body through neural signals?



B-NEURA does not attempt to claim that consciousness can currently be transferred into VR.



Instead, it explores a future interaction model in which the brain could communicate with a virtual environment and potentially receive feedback from that environment.



The core concept is a closed-loop system:



BRAIN  

↓  

BCI / NEURAL SIGNAL ACQUISITION  

↓  

AI DECODER  

↓  

USER INTENTION  

↓  

VIRTUAL BODY  

↓  

VR ENVIRONMENT  

↓  

SENSORY / NEURAL FEEDBACK  

↓  

BRAIN



The website should make this concept understandable to a non-technical audience while maintaining scientific credibility.



\---



\# 2. CORE RESEARCH QUESTION



Primary research question:



> Can a brain-computer interface combined with virtual reality create a sense of agency and virtual embodiment for people with severe paralysis?



Secondary research questions:



> Could future sensory-feedback systems make interaction with a virtual body feel more natural?



> Could a virtual body eventually provide a meaningful alternative pathway for physical interaction?



> How might BCI, AI, and VR work together as a closed-loop system?



These questions are exploratory.



The project must NOT claim that the prototype proves these questions.



\---



\# 3. TARGET USER



The conceptual primary user is a person with severe paralysis who retains cognitive function and wants to interact with digital environments despite significant physical limitations.



The project should represent people with paralysis respectfully.



Do not describe paralysis as making someone "less human."



The purpose of the technology is to explore agency, communication, interaction, accessibility, and embodiment.

\# 8. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE



The conceptual architecture contains the following components.



\## 8.1 Brain



The user generates neural activity associated with intention.



Example:



"I want to move my right hand."



The prototype does not collect real neural data.



The website simulates the process.



\---



\## 8.2 BCI



The Brain-Computer Interface represents the layer responsible for detecting and interpreting brain activity.



Potential real-world approaches include technologies such as EEG and other BCI approaches.



The website should explain that different BCI technologies have different levels of signal quality, invasiveness, accuracy, and practical limitations.



\---



\## 8.3 AI Decoder



The AI layer interprets patterns from neural signals.



Conceptual pipeline:



NEURAL SIGNAL  

↓  

SIGNAL PROCESSING  

↓  

FEATURE EXTRACTION  

↓  

AI / ML DECODER  

↓  

INTENTION



Example:



Input:



Neural activity



Output:



"Move right hand"



The website should simulate this process.



\---



\## 8.4 Virtual Body



The user's intention is represented by a virtual body.



The virtual body can contain:



\- hands

\- arms

\- head

\- body

\- movement

\- interaction with objects



The virtual body is important because B-NEURA is not only about controlling a cursor.



The project explores the idea of virtual embodiment.



\---



\## 8.5 VR Environment



The virtual environment provides a space where the user can interact.



The environment should be visually simple enough to maintain performance.



Possible environment:



\- futuristic rehabilitation room

\- calm natural environment

\- laboratory

\- abstract neural environment



The environment should contain interactive objects.



\---



\## 8.6 Sensory Feedback



Sensory feedback represents a future layer in which information about virtual interaction could potentially be returned to the user's nervous system.



Examples:



\- touch

\- pressure

\- position / proprioception

\- vibration

\- temperature



The website must clearly distinguish conceptual neural feedback from currently available VR haptics.



The prototype may visually simulate the feedback process.



It must NOT claim that the website can generate real neural sensations.
